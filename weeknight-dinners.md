# Weeknight Dinners for Dads

A working collection of the meals I actually make, most weeks, for three kids aged 4, 6 and 9.

Not a cookbook. There's no photography, no "nestled on a bed of," no thirty-minute meals that take fifty. These are the things that survived contact with real weeknights — the ones I've made enough times that I don't think about them anymore.

Everything here is kosher, metric, and Celsius.

---

# The principles

The recipes matter less than these do. If you take nothing else, take this section.

## 1. Time is the whole game

Not "quick for a home-cooked meal." Actually fast. The clock starts when you walk in the door tired and it ends when food is in front of a kid who has been asking since 4pm.

Anything that requires you to have made a decision last night has already failed. **No defrosting the night before. No marinating. No prep.** If a recipe's first line is "the evening before," it doesn't belong in a weeknight rotation — it belongs in a different life.

This is why so much of what follows is *from frozen* and *in an air fryer*. Frozen individually-sealed fish fillets go from freezer to plate in twelve minutes with no thawing step. That's not a compromise. That's the point.

## 2. Hands-off beats fast

A twelve-minute recipe that needs you standing over it is worse than a twenty-five-minute one you can walk away from.

You are not only cooking. You are also doing homework, breaking up a fight, and finding someone's other shoe. **Optimize for how much of the cooking time you can spend not cooking.** The air fryer and the pressure cooker both win here — you load them, you close them, you leave.

Where a recipe *does* have two active parts, run them in parallel. See the mac and cheese: the sauce is the slow half, so it starts the moment the pasta hits the water, not when the pasta is done. That single habit turns a 25-minute meal into a 15-minute one.

## 3. A carb and a protein

That's the whole nutritional model. Don't overbuild it.

Carbs are easy — kids eat carbs enthusiastically and without negotiation. **The protein is the entire problem.** So the structure of every meal here is: pick a protein that they'll actually eat, then bolt on whatever carb is fastest. Rice, pasta, ptitim, fries, toast. Toast counts. Toast has always counted.

Vegetables get their own rule: **cut them into finger food and put them on the table raw, with a dip.** Sticks, coins, spears — the shape matters more than you'd think. Techina or roasted garlic dip does more for vegetable consumption in my house than any amount of cooking them does. A cooked vegetable is a battle; a raw carrot stick next to a bowl of techina is just there, and it disappears.

## 4. The kids have to actually eat it

A perfectly balanced meal that comes back untouched has zero nutritional value. This is obvious and everyone forgets it.

Which means the honest feedback stays in these recipes. Where something failed, I say so. You'll see notes like *"kids said burned"* and *"didn't want to try it"* — those are the most useful lines in the document. A recipe collection with no failures in it is lying to you.

---

## 5. Run a weekly default

Decide once what you eat on each night of the week. Then stop deciding.

Monday is pasta. Tuesday is fish and rice. Whatever it is. The point isn't the specific menu — the point is that **the question "what's for dinner" stops being a question.** Decision fatigue at 5:30pm is real, and it's the thing that turns into ordering out for the third time this week.

You're allowed to change your mind. It's a *default*, not a contract. The rule is just: unless something's going on, we're eating this. Having to actively override a plan is a completely different mental load than having to invent one from nothing, every single day, forever.

## 6. Repetition builds muscle memory

This is the part nobody tells you, and it compounds.

It isn't only that an easy recipe is easy. It's that **the fifteenth time you make something, you're not really making it anymore.** You're not measuring, not reading, not checking the next step. Your hands know where things are. You've got the pan out before you've consciously decided to.

So making the same eight things over and over isn't a failure of imagination — it's the actual strategy. Variety is expensive: every new recipe costs you attention you don't have. A small rotation, run often, eventually takes up almost no mental space at all. That's the goal. Not an impressive repertoire. **A repertoire that's free.**

## How to use this

Everything's grouped by what job it does, not by ingredient:

- **Carbs & vegetables** — the easy half. Pick one, pair with anything below.
- **Protein from frozen** — no thaw, no prep, weeknight core.
- **One-pot meals** — carb and protein together, minimum dishes.
- **Batch and freeze** — the one thing worth an hour, once.
- **Breakfast for dinner** — a legitimate and underrated category.

Temperatures are Celsius, weights are metric. Where I use a specific machine I've said so, but nothing here is machine-specific — an air fryer is an air fryer.

# Carbs & vegetables

## Cut vegetables (not a recipe)

Cut whatever raw vegetables you have into finger-food shapes. Cucumber coins, carrot sticks, pepper strips, cherry tomatoes. Put them on the table with a dip before anything else is ready.

This is the single highest-return thing in this document and it takes four minutes. Hungry kids waiting for dinner will eat raw vegetables that the same kids would refuse as a side dish twenty minutes later.

### Techina

Parve.

- 120 ml raw tahini paste
- 60–120 ml water
- 1 Tbsp lemon juice
- 2 cloves garlic
- ½ tsp salt
- 1 tsp parsley flakes (optional)

Everything in the food processor, blend well. Add water gradually — it seizes up and goes pale before it loosens, which is normal and alarming the first time.

### Roasted garlic dip

Parve.

- 4 heads garlic, peeled
- Olive oil
- Coarse salt

Peel the garlic into a roasting pan, drizzle with oil, sprinkle with salt. Roast until soft enough to mash with a fork. Mash and mix. Serve warm or at room temperature.

## Fries — air fryer

Parve. ~23 min total, mostly unattended.

- ~900g potatoes
- 2 Tbsp oil
- Spices to taste

1. Preheat air fryer to **200°C** (3 min).
2. Cut potatoes thin — matchstick or thin slices.
3. Rinse and soak in cold water ~10 min, then dry well. *This is the step that separates real fries from sad ones. Don't skip the drying.*
4. Toss with oil and spices.
5. Load the trays: one gently loaded, one overloaded.
6. Cook **12 min** at 200°C.
7. Redistribute evenly between trays.
8. Cook another **5 min**.

**Pairs with:** anything. Cottage cheese for the protein if you want dairy. Schnitzel, hot dogs, burgers, fish.

## Potato chunks — air fryer

Parve. ~25 min.

- 800–1000g potatoes, cut into 3–4 cm chunks (no peeling)
- 15 ml oil
- 5g salt, 2g black pepper, 2g garlic powder
- Optional: paprika or dried parsley

1. Cut potatoes into even chunks.
2. Toss with oil and seasonings.
3. Single layer in the basket.
4. Air fry **200°C for 20–25 min**, shaking at 10 and 15 minutes.
5. Fork test; add 2–3 min if needed.

**Notes from making it:** Mine didn't eat these with pepper and garlic powder — plain with just salt went better. Also came out looking burned, so consider slightly less time or a bit more oil.

---

## Baby potatoes — air fryer

Parve. ~20 min, zero effort.

1. Poke holes in each potato.
2. Air fry **190°C for 20+ min**, no oil.

**General air fryer baking guide:** 190°C for 18–25 min. If things brown too fast, drop to 160°C for 25 min.

## Rice — stovetop

Parve. ~30 min, unattended after the first two.

**Ratio: 1 cup rice to 1.5 cups water** (Persian long-grain).

1. Rinse rice under cold water until it mostly runs clear.
2. Combine rice, water, pinch of salt.
3. Rapid boil over medium-high.
4. Drop to **lowest heat**, cover tightly.
5. Simmer undisturbed ~15 min. **Holes appearing on the surface means it's nearly there.** A thermometer reading ~98°C means the water's boiling off.
6. Heat off, leave covered **10 min**.
7. Fluff with a fork.

## Ptitim (Israeli couscous)

Parve. ~15 min.

| Amount | Oil | Water |
|---|---|---|
| 1 cup (240 ml) | 1 Tbsp | 1¼ cups (300 ml) |
| Full packet | 2 Tbsp | 3⅓ cups (800 ml) |

1. Fry the ptitim briefly in oil — salt and season to taste.
2. Add boiling water.
3. Partially cover, **low heat 6 min**, stirring occasionally.
4. Cover and rest **5 min** before serving.

The initial fry is what keeps them separate instead of gluey. Worth the ninety seconds.

---

## Corn on the cob

Parve. 10–15 min.

1. Large pot of salted water to a boil.
2. Drop in the ears.
3. Boil 8–10 min.
4. Drizzle with olive oil and salt.

No butter needed. Kids can hold the whole cob without holders — just let them gnaw at it.

**Notes from making it:** mixed reception here. One had a little, one tasted it and passed, one wouldn't try it at all. Your mileage may vary; it was worth one attempt.

## Eggplant — air fryer

Parve. ~30 min including the salting rest.

- 1 large eggplant (~450g)
- ¾ tsp salt, divided
- 2 Tbsp oil
- 1 tsp each: Italian seasoning, garlic powder, paprika

1. Cut into ½ cm slices or 2.5 cm cubes.
2. Lay on paper towels, sprinkle with ½ tsp salt, rest 15 min to draw out moisture.
3. Pat dry thoroughly.
4. Brush or toss with oil, add the seasonings and remaining salt.
5. Preheat air fryer to **200°C**.
6. Single layer, don't crowd. **~12 min**, flipping halfway.
7. Done when a knife slides in easily.

# Protein from frozen

The weeknight core. Nothing here needs thawing.

## Fish fillets — air fryer, honey glaze

Parve. 15 min from frozen. **This is the fastest real dinner in the collection.**

Works for tilapia, salmon, or similar fillets — straight from the freezer.

1. Preheat air fryer to **200°C** (3 min).
2. Frozen fillets into the basket, cook **6 min**. (This is the defrost phase.)
3. Open, brush each fillet with **honey or maple syrup + oil + spices**.
4. Cook another **6 min** at 200°C.
5. Serve immediately.

**Why this works:** the glaze is the entire kid-appeal mechanism. Buy fillets individually sealed and this becomes a decision you can make at 5:45pm.

## Breaded fish fillets — air fryer, from frozen

Parve. ~15 min.

- 2–4 frozen fish fillets (tilapia works well)
- 2 Tbsp panko breadcrumbs
- ¼ tsp salt, ⅛ tsp garlic powder
- Oil spray

1. Mix the panko, salt and garlic powder in a small bowl.
2. Season the frozen fillets on both sides.
3. Spray the tops lightly with oil.
4. Still-frozen fillets into the basket.
5. Spoon about a tablespoon of the crumb mix onto each, pressing gently so it sticks.
6. **188°C for 6 min.**
7. Open and spray the tops again — this is what browns the crumbs.
8. **Another 6–8 min**, until crispy outside and opaque and flaky inside.

Don't thaw first. Straight from frozen genuinely works better here.

## Salmon — soy sauce

Parve. Oven, 180°C, 20–25 min. *From my mother.*

- Salmon fillets
- Soy sauce (a few shakes)
- Sesame oil
- Garlic powder
- Ginger powder

No added salt — the soy has it covered.

1. Fillets on a baking sheet.
2. Mix the soy sauce, sesame oil, garlic powder and ginger in a small bowl.
3. Spread over the fillets, front and back. Pour any remainder over the top.
4. **Cover tightly**, bake at 180°C for 20–25 min.

## Salmon — sweet and sour

Parve. Oven, 180°C, 20–25 min. *From my mother.*

- Salmon fillets
- Salt (a little), black pepper, paprika, garlic powder
- Olive oil
- Ginger powder
- Sweet and sour sauce
- Sesame oil (a few drops)

1. Line a pan with foil and baking paper. Lay out the fillets.
2. Sprinkle salt, pepper, paprika and garlic powder on both sides.
3. Drizzle with olive oil, spread with a brush, coating both sides.
4. **Cover tightly with foil**, bake 180°C for 20–25 min, until no longer pink.
5. Out of the oven, clean up the fillets, then sprinkle again with garlic powder, a little salt and ginger.
6. Mix the sweet and sour sauce with a few drops of sesame oil, a little ginger, and some of the pan juices.
7. Spread over the fillets. Serve, or refrigerate for later.

## Hot dogs — air fryer

Meat. 10 min.

1. Preheat to **200°C** (3 min).
2. **Score the hot dogs** with 3–4 diagonal cuts. This matters — they crisp at the cuts and it's the difference between a boiled-tasting hot dog and a good one.
3. Cook **5 min**.
4. Add the bun, cook another **1–2 min**.

**Kid notes:** know your customers. In my house one takes it untoasted and one won't have the bun at all. Ask first; it costs nothing and saves a plate coming back.

---

## Hamburgers — air fryer

Meat. 15 min.

- 500g ground beef → 4 patties, ~2.5 cm thick

1. Preheat to **200°C** (3 min).
2. Shape the patties. **Press a shallow indent into the center of each** — this stops them puffing into meatballs.
3. **190°C for 10 min**, flipping at the 5-minute mark.

**Seasoned version:** add ½ tsp each salt, pepper, garlic powder, onion powder, and 1 tsp smoked paprika to the beef before shaping. Chill 10 min if you have it. Cook to 71°C internal, rest 2 min.

**Turkey version:** 500g ground turkey, 1 beaten egg, 40g breadcrumbs, 1 tsp each garlic and onion powder, ½ tsp each salt, pepper and poultry seasoning, 1 Tbsp olive oil. Mix gently, shape, chill 15 min. 190°C for 7 min, flip, 5–7 more, to **74°C internal.**

## Turkey meatballs — air fryer

Meat. 5 min prep, 10–12 min cook.

- 500g ground turkey
- ½ cup breadcrumbs (panko best; plain or matzah meal fine)
- 1 egg
- **2 Tbsp oil — do not skip this or they'll be dry**
- 1 tsp garlic powder (powder beats fresh here — no chunks to object to)
- ½ tsp salt
- Optional: a squeeze of ketchup or silan into the mix for sweetness and moisture

1. Preheat air fryer to **200°C**.
2. Everything in a bowl, mix with your hands **just until combined**. Over-mixing makes them tough.
3. **Wet your hands** (turkey is sticky) and roll into ping-pong-sized balls.
4. Spray the basket with oil, single layer, don't stack. Spray the tops too.
5. **10–12 min**, shaking the basket at 6 minutes. Thermometer to **74°C**.

**The trick:** with one minute left, brush them with BBQ, teriyaki or sweet chili sauce and let them finish. Sticky and glazed.

**The other trick:** if they're refused on a plate, put a toothpick in each one. Kids will eat almost anything on a stick.

# One-pot meals

Carb and protein together. Minimum dishes, which counts for more than people admit.

## Potato and schnitzel — pressure cooker

Meat. 8 minutes under pressure.

- 400g frozen raw turkey schnitzel (separated pieces)
- 600g potatoes, peeled, 2–3 cm chunks
- 250 ml water or chicken broth
- 1 tsp salt, ½ tsp pepper, 1 tsp garlic powder, 1 tsp paprika

1. Water/broth and seasonings into the pot.
2. Potatoes in, then **layer the schnitzel on top — don't stir it in or submerge it.**
3. Lid on, valve sealed. **Pressure cook HIGH 8 min.**
4. Quick release immediately.
5. Check schnitzel is at **74°C** internal.

Scales to 800g potato / ~500g schnitzel with 300 ml liquid.

## Rice and schnitzel — pressure cooker

Meat. ~20 min.

- 2 cups rice, 2 cups water
- Chicken schnitzel slices (separated)
- ½ tsp each: salt, garlic powder, onion powder

1. Everything into the pressure cooker.
2. **5 min** pressure cook.
3. **10 min** natural release.
4. Check meat internal temp: **74°C**.

## Meat sauce (kosher bolognese)

Meat. ~20 min. Serve over pasta or toast.

- 500g ground beef or turkey
- 1 medium onion, diced
- 1 Tbsp oil
- 1 carrot, finely grated
- 1 zucchini or a handful of mushrooms, grated (optional — hidden)
- 1 cup frozen corn (optional)
- 1 can (800g) diced tomatoes with juice
- 1½ tsp paprika, 1 tsp dried basil, ½ tsp salt, ½ tsp black pepper

1. Sear/sauté on medium. Oil in, onion 2 min.
2. Ground meat, 3–4 min until browned, breaking it up.
3. Stir in the seasonings.
4. Grated vegetables and corn.
5. Canned tomatoes with juice. Sauté 3–4 min more if you want it thicker.
6. Lid on, **pressure cook HIGH 5 min**, quick release.

**Notes from making it — this one didn't land.** Mine didn't want to try it; the verdict was "more like meat and ketchup." I'm keeping it in because the format is right even though my execution wasn't, and because a collection where everything works is a collection that's hiding something. Next attempt: less tomato, and have them help make it — participation moves the needle more than seasoning does.

**Related:** the same thing with turkey and no vegetables is essentially homemade hamburger helper. My note from that attempt: **halve the salt and pepper, and it was way too much tomato.** It seemed done after the sauté stage (already boiling) so I skipped the pressure cook — but it still came out somewhat soggy. Reduce the liquid.

## Hot dogs and potatoes — air fryer

Meat. ~23 min. Serves about 5.

- 700g potatoes, peeled, 4 cm cubes
- 500g hot dogs, cut into 5 cm chunks
- 1 medium red onion, large chunks
- 2 Tbsp olive oil
- ½ tsp salt, ¼ tsp black pepper, ¼ tsp garlic powder, 1 tsp Italian seasoning, 1 tsp dried parsley

1. Toss potatoes, onion, oil and seasonings until coated.
2. **200°C for 15 min**, shaking halfway.
3. Add the hot dog chunks. **Another 8 min**, until potatoes are fork-tender and the hot dogs have charred edges.

Serve with ketchup. Peppers work in here too.

**Notes from making it:** mine called it burned. More oil next time, or a couple fewer minutes.

## Mac and cheese

Dairy. ~15 min if you work in parallel. 4–5 servings.

Israeli-style, with shamenet l'bishul and white cheese.

- 400g pasta (elbow or similar)
- 500 ml shamenet l'bishul (15% cooking cream)
- 250–300g sliced white cheese, torn into pieces
- 2–3 Tbsp butter (optional, richer)
- 2–3 Tbsp flour (optional, thicker sauce)
- ½ tsp salt, pinch black pepper, ¼ tsp garlic powder (optional)

> **Work in parallel — this is the whole trick.** The sauce is the slow part, not the pasta. The moment the pasta is in the boiling water, move to the second burner and start the sauce. Don't wait. Both land together and it's a 15-minute job instead of 25.

1. **Cook pasta** in salted water per the package. Drain, set aside.
2. **Sauce, started while the pasta boils:** melt butter in a large pan over medium. Add flour, whisk ~1 min until it bubbles.
3. Slowly add the shamenet, whisking constantly. Simmer 3–5 min until slightly thickened. **Don't boil.** Salt, pepper, garlic powder.
4. Reduce to low. Add the white cheese in portions, stirring until fully melted and smooth.
5. Add the drained pasta, stir to coat, 2–3 min on low to meld.

Serve hot immediately — it thickens as it sits.

**Notes:** lighter cream is less rich but still fine. Shredded yellow cheese adds flavor. Skip the flour entirely for a thinner, simpler sauce and one less step.

# Batch and freeze

## Schnitzel — oven

Meat. **This is the exception to every rule above.** *From my mother.*

It takes a good hour of actual work plus cooking time. You do it once, you freeze it, and then for weeks afterward schnitzel is a fifteen-minute reheat. That trade is worth making on a Sunday. It is never worth making on a Tuesday.

- A box of schnitzel, cleaned up
- 1–2 eggs (3 for a full box)
- Matzah meal or breadcrumbs
- Garlic granules or powder, paprika, onion granules or powder, parsley
- Two longish bowls

1. Beat an egg in the first bowl.
2. Fill the second bowl with matzah meal or breadcrumbs. Sprinkle in the spices and mix well.
3. Take each piece with a fork, dip in the egg. (A small sprinkle of garlic powder directly onto each piece of chicken or turkey helps.)
4. Into the crumbs, one at a time, coating both sides well.
5. Onto a baking sheet lined with baking paper — smear the paper with olive oil first. Pack them close together.
6. Sprinkle a little oil on the pieces and tap it around with a silicone brush.
7. *If they don't all fit:* lay another sheet of baking paper on top, smear with oil, and put the rest on that. Oil and tap the second layer too. You can skip this second layer, but the result is drier.
8. **Cover the tray tightly with foil.**
9. **Bake at 180°C for 40 min**, or to **74°C** in the thickest part.

Cool, freeze in portions. Reheat from frozen in the air fryer or oven.

## Schnitzel — air fryer

Meat. For when you're making it fresh rather than batching.

- 2 large chicken breasts → 4 fillets
- ⅓ cup flour + ¼ tsp paprika + ½ tsp garlic powder + ¼ tsp each salt and pepper
- 2 eggs, beaten
- 2 cups panko + 1 tsp garlic powder + 1 tsp dried parsley + salt and pepper

1. Preheat air fryer to **200°C**.
2. Slice each breast in half horizontally into 4 thin fillets. Pound to even thickness.
3. Three bowls: seasoned flour, beaten egg, seasoned panko.
4. Coat each fillet: **flour → egg → breadcrumbs.**
5. Spray the basket with oil. Fillets in, spray the tops.
6. **12–15 min**, turning once about three-quarters through.

**Notes from making it:** a large quantity took 6–8 runs through the air fryer, which is exactly why the oven batch version above exists. Oiling the basket and flipping when golden works well. Five minutes before flipping was too crunchy; two minutes was better — check by thickness rather than by clock.

## Hard-boiled eggs — pressure cooker

Parve. 12 min. **The 4/4/4 method.**

1. 1 cup water in the pot, eggs on the rack.
2. **4 min** pressure cook.
3. **4 min** quick release.
4. **4 min** ice bath.

I make a dozen at a time; they keep about a week in the fridge. Instant protein for a lunchbox or a kid who didn't eat dinner.

(The classic version is 5/5/5, also with quick release.)

# Breakfast for dinner

## Pancakes — whole wheat

Dairy. ~20 min.

- 2 cups whole wheat flour
- 1 tsp baking soda
- 1 tsp salt
- 2 Tbsp brown sugar
- 2 cups (500 ml) buttermilk
- 2 eggs
- 2 Tbsp oil

1. Mix the flour, baking soda and salt in a bowl.
2. Make a well in the middle. Add the oil, buttermilk, eggs and sugar. Mix.
3. Using a ¼, ⅓ or ½ cup as a scoop, pour onto a hot oiled griddle.
4. Turn when bubbles form on top, about 2 min. Brown the second side, about 2 min.
5. Serve with syrup, honey, cottage cheese or fruit. **Cottage cheese is how this becomes a protein.**

### Getting a good rise

- **Fold, don't stir**, once wet and dry are combined. Mixing hard pops the CO₂ bubbles. Lumps are fine.
- **Cook within 5 min of mixing.** The baking soda reacts immediately and batter that sits loses its lift. I've lost batches to this.
- **Heat high enough** — medium-high, so the inside dries properly. Too low gives you a gummy center.

You can double this (except the baking soda). Put a timer on and clean the kitchen while you flip.
